The short version

There is no global welfare census.

But several conclusions survive that limitation. Many nonhuman animals have the capacity for positive and negative experience. Human systems affect an immense number of them. Measures of production and mortality reveal scale, while a smaller body of welfare research documents pain, disease, restriction, fear, frustration, and—in some settings—opportunities for positive experience.

The evidence is least complete where the numbers may be largest: aquatic animals, invertebrates, and free-living wild animals. A responsible picture of the world therefore combines what is measured with a clear account of what remains unknown.

How to read the evidence

A count is not an experience.

Animal welfare is about how life feels to an individual. The available evidence approaches that question through several imperfect lenses.

  1. 01

    Who can experience?

    Behavior, neurobiology, cognition, and evolutionary continuity inform judgments about sentience. Uncertainty is not the same as evidence of absence.

  2. 02

    How many are affected?

    Official counts and modeled estimates establish scale. They do not, by themselves, establish the quality of the lives counted.

  3. 03

    What happens to them?

    Housing, handling, capture, disease, hunger, injury, slaughter, and ecology describe conditions that can generate different experiences.

  4. 04

    How bad, and for how long?

    Severity, duration, and prevalence turn a list of hazards into a welfare assessment. This is the most important—and sparsest—layer.

Method & reuse

Built to be read—by people and machines.

Last reviewed {{UPDATED}}. This is a selective first edition, not a claim to completeness.

01

Selection

We favor domains with substantial, durable material; clear methods or citations; a distinct contribution; and pages or data that can be accessed without an account. Quality outranks quantity.

02

Evidence labels

Direct welfare observations, official counts, modeled counts, pressure proxies, sentience evidence, synthesis, and normative argument answer different questions. The cards identify rather than flatten those differences.

03

No mirrored content

No linked site’s prose, charts, datasets, or publisher abstracts are copied here. The summaries are original. JSON and CSV expose this atlas’s own catalog metadata—not a copy of anyone else’s site.

04

Crawlable ≠ reusable

A page being technically accessible does not grant permission to train on, republish, or redistribute it. Each source keeps its own rights. Access notes and reuse notes are deliberately separate.

Structured access

Use the catalog in the format you need.

The full visible text is static HTML. The same original catalog metadata is also available as JSON, CSV, JSON-LD, and llms.txt. Crawling is permitted in robots.txt.
