The state of animal welfare

Humans slaughter more than 80 billion land animals and an estimated 130 billion farmed fish each year. The number of wild-caught fish is measured in trillions.

Animal sentience

The New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness reports strong scientific support for conscious experience in mammals and birds. It also finds a realistic possibility of conscious experience in all vertebrates and many invertebrates, including cephalopods, decapod crustaceans, and insects.

Animals capable of pain, fear, comfort, frustration, or pleasure have lives that can go well or badly. Relevant evidence comes from neurobiology, behavior, cognition, and evolutionary relationships between species.

Sources: New York Declaration, Searching for Animal Sentience, Animal Consciousness, Animal Sentience

The scale

  • More than 80 billion land animals are slaughtered for farming each year.
  • About 130 billion farmed finfish were slaughtered in 2022, with an estimated range of 86–180 billion.
  • 1.1–2.2 trillion wild fish were caught annually on average from 2000 to 2019.

Chickens account for most slaughtered land animals. In 2021, about 74 billion chickens were killed for meat: roughly 202 million per day or 140,000 per minute.

Land-animal totals largely come from country reports collected by the UN Food and Agriculture Organization. Fish counts are modeled from records reported in tonnes, so their uncertainty is wider.

An estimated 74% of farmed land animals live in factory farms. At any given time, this is about 23 billion animals.

Sources: Our World in Data, FAO, Fishcount, Animal Clock

Conditions

Welfare depends on the severity and duration of experiences as well as the number of animals affected. Common harms in intensive farming include disease, injury, lameness, chronic hunger, heat stress, confinement, rough handling, and painful slaughter.

Modern broiler chickens grow more than 400% faster than chickens raised in the 1950s and reach slaughter weight in five to six weeks. Rapid growth contributes to skeletal pain and lameness. Research summarized by Our World in Data estimates that slower-growing breeds could reduce time in disabling pain by 67% and excruciating pain by 80%.

The Welfare Footprint Project estimates time spent in pain at different intensities. This makes it possible to compare changes in the prevalence, severity, and duration of suffering.

Sources: Welfare Footprint, Faunalytics, EU ALURES

Aquatic animals

Fisheries usually report weight rather than individual animals. Fishcount converts fisheries and aquaculture tonnage into estimated numbers of fish. Its estimates suggest that farmed finfish may outnumber slaughtered land animals and that wild-caught fish outnumber them many times over.

Capture and slaughter can cause exhaustion, crushing, decompression injuries, exposure to air, and prolonged death by asphyxiation or chilling.

Sea Around Us reconstructs marine catch to include discards, small-scale fishing, and unreported catch missing from official landings.

Sources: Fishcount, Sea Around Us, FAO Fisheries

Wild animals

Life in nature contains suffering on an enormous scale. Wild animals endure disease, parasites, hunger, thirst, untreated injuries, temperature extremes, predation, and the loss of offspring.

Most wild animals do not survive to adulthood. In species that produce many offspring, stable populations can coexist with vast numbers of brief lives ending in starvation, disease, exposure, or predation.

Conservation tracks populations and species. Wild-animal welfare asks what happens to the individuals within them. A species can thrive while many of its members suffer and die young.

Some of this suffering is already preventable through vaccination, safer infrastructure, humane population management, and treatment of animals harmed by human activity. As ecological knowledge and our ability to intervene improve, the preventable share could grow. Wild Animal Initiative develops welfare measures needed to guide interventions, while the Wild Animal Welfare Committee applies welfare evidence to wildlife management.

Sources: Wild Animal Initiative, Wild Animal Welfare Committee

Possible futures

Better measurement, changes to intensive farming, and greater attention to aquatic and wild animals could prevent severe suffering. Some researchers and writers consider more fundamental changes:

  • The Hedonistic Imperative argues that biotechnology could eventually reduce or abolish suffering in humans and nonhuman animals.
  • Herbivorize Predators proposes directed evolution to allow predatory species to survive without killing other animals.
  • Sentience Institute studies how societies extend moral concern to animals and possible digital minds.
  • Center for Reducing Suffering studies futures containing severe suffering and ways to prevent them.
  • Essays on Reducing Suffering covers wild animals, insects, artificial minds, and long-term outcomes.
  • Qualia Research Institute researches formal theories of consciousness and the positive or negative character of experience.
